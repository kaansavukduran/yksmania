# Accepted direction and remaining stages

1. First vertical slice: real original lessons, retrieval, assisted/independent
   evidence, local state, availability, day-ordinal reviews, source atlas, PDF
   import and offline builds. Six topics and thirty questions are the initial scope.
2. Private content inventory: complete recursive file and edition/page/question
   matching of the user's Aurelia YKS archive. Catalogue presence is not verified
   content readiness. Geometry portions inside mathematics books need mapping.
3. Curated topic packs for every TYT subject and numerical AYT, with source rights,
   answer-key verification, prerequisite links and official examination-year scope.
4. More teaching activities: fading examples, diagnostic hints, repair questions,
   interleaving, new-item transfer checks and delayed independent assessment pools.
5. Full exam mode and paper-test answer entry, official format verification, timing,
   item-level error classification and longitudinal analysis. No ranking promises.
6. Adaptive information-card scheduler only after calibration; preserve the current
   accepted 1/3/7/21 history and offer a reviewable migration. Skill transfer remains
   separate from card-recall prediction.
7. Native production signing, installer/portable clean-launch checks, real Android
   and Windows validation, two-package update/data preservation checks. Cloud sync
   is optional future work; no implied continuous background service.
8. Richer AIONIA discoveries and optionally cooperative sessions after verifying
   their benefit and obtaining specific character/lore decisions.

Personal use is the initial working assumption. Public account infrastructure,
subscriptions, chat, leaderboards and importing personal historical records are
not silently introduced. Local settings and learning data remain device-scoped.

## Learning evidence and product claims

The implemented cycle asks the learner to retrieve an answer, gives explanatory
feedback and schedules another attempt on a later date. Assisted responses remain
separate from independent answers. Confidence is learner self-report, not mastery.
The accepted day 1/3/7/21 sequence is a configurable-product starting convention;
research does not establish it as the best schedule for every learner or subject.

Relevant foundations for subsequent content design:

- Dunlosky et al. (2013), practice testing and distributed practice review:
  https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
- Roediger & Karpicke (2006), delayed retention after retrieval practice:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x
- Cepeda et al. (2006), quantitative synthesis of distributed practice:
  https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354

These findings support design choices; they are not evidence that this alpha
improves YKS scores. Evaluate delayed independent performance and new-item transfer,
not XP, session counts or repeated exposure to the same five questions.
